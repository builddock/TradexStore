Capabilities

Every switchable piece of behaviour the platform has. A capability decides whether a store has a thing at all; permissions inside a store decide who may use it. A capability that is off leaves nothing behind — no menu entry, no page, no code sent to the browser, and no hint that it exists.

About 230 features across 18 areas

Communication channels and automations are the features clients most often buy separately, so each one is its own switch. Granting a channel also requires a provider account, a verified sender and approved templates — the store configuration cannot be published until those exist.

Six places a switched-off capability is enforced

  1. Navigation — the entry is not in the menu and the address is not routed
  2. Interface — the endpoint answers “not found”, never “not allowed”
  3. Inside the system — internal callers and background jobs are refused too
  4. Screens — the component is not rendered and its code is not sent to the browser
  5. Automations — the job is never scheduled; queued work is parked with a reason
  6. Data out — nothing appears in exports, search, reports or notifications

Rules

Not found, not forbidden. “Not allowed” would confirm the feature exists. Anything a store does not have simply is not there.
Nothing is deleted. Switching a capability off hides its data. Switching it back on restores everything, unchanged.
Dependencies resolve, conflicts block. Turning on a capability turns on what it needs and says so. A combination that cannot work cannot be published.
Locking is invisible. A locked capability does not appear as disabled in the store's own admin — it is absent.
